# Dresses (PR 5) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Fit and draw dresses. A dress is a new `dress` template in a new `onepiece` slot. It scores the top half (bust, plus shoulder and long sleeve when charted) and the bottom half (waist at the seam, hips, where the hem lands, walking room). In the Fitting room it replaces the top and the bottom, and a jacket can go over it.

**Architecture:**
- **Fit.** A pure `domain/fit/dress.ts` places the dress on the body: the seam height, the hem height (high point of the shoulder minus the HPS length), and the HPS-to-knee distance the hem lands against.
- `DRESS_ZONES` in `zones.ts` reuse the existing rules. Bust and waist take the top chest bands, hips take the bottom hips band. Shoulder takes the jacket band, sleeve takes the knit/jacket sleeve zone. Length takes the skirt's landing marks, walking room takes the skirt's stride check.
- **New number.** The only new body number is the HPS height, `hpsHeightCm`. It moves the drawing's existing `0.165 × H` into `domain/` unchanged, so no existing drawing moves.
- **Drawing.** `templates/dress.ts` composes the unchanged `topShape` for the bodice, with its hem at the seam, and a skirt outline from the seam to the engine's hem height. That skirt outline is factored out of `skirtShape` behind a snapshot guard.
- **Outfit.** The outfit gets a flat `onepieceId/onepieceSize` (outfit and outfits v3). `fitOutfit` fits a jacket over the dress exactly as it fits one over a top.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, React, Vitest. Run everything from `app/`.

**Spec:** `docs/clothing-categories-design.md` §3.2 (the onepiece slot) and §6 "PR 5 — dresses"; "PR 3 as built" (skirt landing, walking room); "PR 4 as built" (layering); plus the task brief.

## Global Constraints

- Every new threshold or ratio is labelled **ASSUMED** in its doc comment. That covers the HPS height ratio, the dress ranges, and the choice to reuse the top chest bands for a dress bust and waist.
- Every existing golden line (`src/domain/fit/__golden__/*`) stays byte-identical. The two dresses only **add** lines. Check with `git diff --stat` and `git diff -U0 … | grep '^-[^-]'`, which must print nothing.
- Existing verdict wording doesn't change. New wording is only new keys (a `dress` noun, and unknown-reason text for the waist seam).
- Skin rule: the dress's bust, seam, hips and hem are drawn at `domain/` heights and widths (`dress.ts`, `skirt.ts`, the chart via `halfWidth`). The bodice is `topShape`, as for every top.
- No new `BodyMeasurements` keys, so the sampler's streams are untouched. `hpsHeightCm` is a function of height.
- No product photos: the dress template has `landmarks: null`. Neither sample row has an `image`.
- Storage shape changes bump the key (`outfit`, `outfits` → v3). Migrate in `ui/state/migrate.ts`.
- `npm run check` must exit 0 before every commit. Don't commit `node_modules`, `.models/`, `chart-snapshots/` or other caches.

## Decisions — please confirm or change (★ = my recommendation)

1. **How a dress fills the outfit.** ★ It gets a new `onepiece` category and its own flat fields, `onepieceId` / `onepieceSize`, beside top / bottom / outer (outfit v3, outfits v3).
   - Wearing a dress empties `top` and `bottom`.
   - Wearing a top or a bottom takes the dress off, and the other empty slot refills with its default (`firstGarment`), as on a fresh start.
   - The previous top and bottom aren't remembered under the dress.
   - The jacket slot is untouched. A jacket over a dress is fitted over the dress (the dress is its `under` layer), and the readout says "With your dress under it".
   - The Fitting room gets a fourth tab, **Dress**. It shows when the catalogue on show has a dress or one is worn. With a men's body on "men's & unisex" there are none, so the tab hides.
2. **Personal fit.** ★ Dresses are **objective only** (`personal: null`), as pants and skirts are. The anchor is a knit tee, and a woven bodice is cut differently, with no sourced offset to bridge them. The alternative is personal on the bust with a dress ease offset, which would be one more assumed number.
3. **Bust and waist bands.** ★ Reuse **`TOP_CHEST` (−1 / +2 / +4 in) for every dress sizing**, labelled ASSUMED for dresses, rather than `WOMENS_TOP_CHEST` (−1 / +1 / +3 in). The sheath chart below gives +4 in of bust ease at each size's own body chest. The women's tee bands would call every size "loose" on the body it's cut for. Hips use `BOTTOM_HIPS`, as skirts do.
4. **The waist seam** (`chart.definitions.waistSeam`: `natural | empire | drop | none | unknown`):
   - `natural` and `none` (a sheath fitted by darts) compare the waist at the navel, the body's waist as ANSUR II measures it. That's the same place a skirt's `natural` band sits.
   - `empire`, `drop` and `unknown` leave the waist **unscored**, with a note. Empire needs an underbust (PR 6, on hold); drop has no body number yet.
   - **Waist and hips are `optional`**: when they can't be scored, the size isn't "unknown" because of them. A fit-and-flare chart often has no hip row. When charted and scored, they count as any girth does.
   - The **bust is required**.
5. **Hem.** The length is a landing zone with the skirt's marks (mini / above knee / knee / midi / maxi, wording reused), against HPS → mid-knee.
   - It accepts `hps`, and `center_back` + `BACK_NECK_DROP_CM`.
   - Walking room is the skirt's check at the dress's hem: optional, warn-only, never decides the size.
   - The length runs straight down. A front length over a full bust would hang a little shorter; that isn't modelled, and the doc comment says so.
6. **Sample data** (tier B, Lands' End item-dimension PDFs, "actual garment measurements in inches", Regular sizes only; Petite/Tall/Plus left out, as for the A-line skirt):
   - **Women's Washable Wool Piped Sheath Dress**, [505999](https://www.landsend.com/garment_measurements/505999_core_pdp_spec.pdf). Sizes 4–18. Rows: garment chest, waist, hip, length centre back, sleeve (top of sleeve, short), sweep. All circumference: chest 38 on a 34 body. `waistSeam: none`. The sweep is smaller than the hip, a pencil cut, so walking room will warn (there's no vent field).
   - **Women's Pattern Knit Shirred Surplice Dress**, [441021](https://www.landsend.com/garment_measurements/441021_core_pdp_spec.pdf). Sizes XS–XL. Rows: garment chest, length HPS, sleeve (top shoulder, short), sweep. No waist and no hip row. `waistSeam` from the product page (`natural` if it says so, else `unknown`). Stretch from the fabric content on the product page.
   - Not used: the sleeveless fit-and-flare, 444488. Its chest is "back only", which can't be made a full girth, and it's sleeveless (see 7). The empire dress, 430338, has a waist we couldn't score.
7. **Sleeveless dresses are parked.** `sleeve` stays `short | long` and is required on a dress. A `none` value would mean teaching `topShape` to draw no sleeves; that's a follow-up.

2. **A maxi on a short body (150 cm), or a long dress on a tall body (200 cm):** the hem height may go below 0. The fit still lands "maxi", and the drawing clamps the hem above the floor, as `skirtShape` does. Tests in Tasks 2 and 6.

4. **Stored v2 outfits and saved outfits** load as v3 with an empty dress slot. A v3 outfit with a dress and a jacket restores both, with the top and bottom empty. Tests in Task 5.
5. **A jacket over a dress** gets no "hem shows below" note: the dress is always longer. The dress's spare bust ease still counts toward the jacket's room, and its pins move to the jacket. Tests in Tasks 5 and 6.
