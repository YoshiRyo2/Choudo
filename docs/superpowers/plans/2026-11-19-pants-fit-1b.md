## Decisions this plan makes (please confirm or change)

1. **Where the band sits.** §2.6 writes `bandHeight = crotchHeight + 0.71 × frontRise`. I propose anchoring at the navel instead:
   `bandHeight(body, garmentRise) = waistHeightCm − RISE_VERTICAL_RATIO × (frontRiseCm − garmentRise)`.
   At the ANSUR II mean the two formulas give the same answer: navel − crotch is 19.8 cm vertically, the front crotch length is 27.9 cm, and 19.8 / 27.9 = 0.709. The difference is that this version uses the body's own front rise, which grows with weight (belly), so a given rise sits lower on a heavier body. It also makes the rise zone an ordinary garment − body difference (garment rise − your front rise), with band − navel = 0.71 × that difference.
   `RISE_VERTICAL_RATIO`: women's 0.71 (ANSUR II female); men's 0.79 (the ANSUR II male means, labelled assumed until the men's re-fit).
2. **Men's girth keys.** You wrote "ratios of height". For the heights (waist/navel, hip, knee), the front rise and the heel–ankle I'll do exactly that. For **thigh, knee and calf**, a ratio of height ignores weight: a 120 kg man would get the same thigh as a 60 kg one. I propose ratios of the existing men's **hips** estimate instead (it already scales with BMI): thigh 0.61, knee 0.40, calf 0.385. Every ratio comes from the ANSUR II male means (mean key / mean stature, or / mean seat), read from the male CSV in the scratchpad only, and is labelled assumed. Men's existing keys don't change.
3. **The hip line** is ANSUR's `buttockheight`, the height of the fullest part of the seat. That's where `hipsCm` (the buttock circumference) is measured, so the waist-to-hips girth interpolation and the drawing's hip line use the same height.
4. **Blocking landing zones** reuse labels that already exist, so no new label or verdict is added and tops' verdict objects keep their keys. A leg opening that can't pass the heel gets `tight` (verdict "too tight"). A band below the hip line gets `short` (verdict "shorter"). Both have tone `bad` and a note.
5. **Thigh, rise and leg opening aren't required.** A pants chart without them, or without the definition they need, shows those zones as "Can't tell". The size's overall result doesn't turn unknown because of them (§6 commit 5). Waist and hips stay required.
6. **The relation check.** §2.5's `thigh < hips / 2 + 8` flags ordinary relaxed jeans when used with full circumferences (a typical thigh is about 0.62 × the seat). I propose `thigh ≤ 0.7 × hips` (a warning) instead, plus `legOpening ≤ knee ≤ thigh`.
7. **Seed for the done check.** I'll give both Corvo placeholder jeans (men's) a thigh, knee and leg opening, `definitions: { rise: 'front', thigh: 'below_crotch_2_5cm' }`. They stay placeholders ("Sample data"). **Question:** is your body set to men's or women's? With a women's body these men's jeans only show under "All". If you'd like a real row too (a brand spec sheet that lists thigh, front rise and leg opening, added by hand per `docs/adding-garments.md`), say which brand or cut and I'll add it to 1b-1.
8. **Sampler streams.** The main random stream is frozen to the 7 body keys and 8 garment keys it draws today. Each key added from now on is drawn from its own stream, seeded from `(SAMPLER_SEED, key name)`. That's why tops stay byte-identical, and why future keys never move anything. CLAUDE.md's "add new keys at the end" rule gets rewritten to match.
9. **Pants landmarks**: all 14 from §2.4 are required, including the knee points. The picker suggests them all, so optional points (and interpolating without them) aren't worth the extra code yet.
