**Papers**

1. **FIT: A Large-Scale Dataset for Fit-Aware Virtual Try-On (Karras et al., 2026).** This is the most relevant recent paper. Its authors point out that the absence of datasets with precise garment and body size information is the main obstacle, and that current try-on models default to generating well-fitted results regardless of the garment or person size. It is a SIGGRAPH 2026 paper, and the authors say they will release the data and code, so check the project page for what’s available. Read it for the framing and for how they handle the measurement problem. [papers](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2604.08526)
2. **A Deep Learning System for Predicting Size and Fit in Fashion E-Commerce (Sheikh et al., Zalando Research, 2019).** This covers the sizing side. The authors build on personalized size and fit recommendations, which drive customer satisfaction and reduce the cost of size-related returns. Their data is proprietary, so the method is more useful to learn from than the results. [harvard](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019arXiv190709844S/abstract)
3. **Anthropometrical Virtual Try-on: A Survey on Virtual Try-ons and Human Body Dimension Estimation (Jain et al., 2022).** This is the closest match to your idea. The authors note that research combining body dimension estimation with try-on to show what a specific garment in a specific size would look like is scarce, which is the gap you’d be filling. It’s a good map of the related work. [eudl](https://eudl.eu/pdf/10.4108/eai.16-4-2022.2318169)
4. **A Rule-Based Framework for Clothing Fit Recommendation from 3D Body Reconstruction (IJACSA, 2025).** This is the most practical one for a two-person team. It takes body measurements from a single image and classifies them into standardized sizes (XS–XL) using a transparent rule-based approach. Its limitations section is a useful checklist for your v1. [thesai](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume16No6/Paper_45-A_Rule_Based_Framework_for_Clothing_Fit_Recommendation.pdf)
5. **SMPL: A Skinned Multi-Person Linear Model (Loper et al., 2015).** This is the body model paper behind the avatar question. SMPL is a realistic 3D model of the human body that is based on skinning and blend shapes and is learned from thousands of 3D body scans. Read it before you pick an avatar approach. The SMPL site also lists SMPL-X, which adds articulated hands and an expressive face, and STAR as a drop-in replacement. [mpg](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/)
6. **Survey: You can Try without Visiting (Ghodhbani et al., 2022).** This covers the image-based try-on literature broadly, so skim it for context rather than method.
