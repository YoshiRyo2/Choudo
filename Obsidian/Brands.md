**Strong candidates**

- **Lands’ End.** Its spec sheets are explicit. They say the vertical numbers below each size are the actual garment measurements in inches and list garment chest, length, and sleeve for the Men’s Super-T crewneck. This is the best fit for your data needs. [landsend](https://www.landsend.com/garment_measurements/426768_core_pdp_spec.pdf)
- **Muji.** Its tee chart has columns for Chest Width, Shoulder Width, Hem Width, Sleeve Length, Length. Those look like garment measures, but the snippet doesn’t say whether chest is flat, so check the page. [muji](https://www.muji.us/pages/apparel-size-chart)
- **Uniqlo.** Already on your list. Its product size chart is the finished-garment measurement, which is what you need. [Uniqlo](https://www.uniqlo.com/uk/en/special-feature/measurement)

**Backups with explicit garment numbers**

- **Knightweave.** Its chart says every figure below is a measurement of the tee itself, not of the body. It’s a small brand, so check whether its chart stays stable over time. [knightweave](https://knightweave.com/pages/size-guide-tees)
- **Everybody World.** Its tees list garment chest width and length, but note that measurements are approximate and may deviate 1" due to variances in the garment dye process. [everybody](https://everybody.world/en-ca/products/heather-short-sleeve-tee)

**Not a good fit**

- **Patagonia.** Its charts state that measurements refer to body size, not garment dimensions, so they won’t work as garment data. [theoutfitters](https://theoutfitters.nf.ca/products/patagonia-spoke-stencil-responsibili-tee-mens-37605)
- **Everlane.** Its snippets show chest ranges like 34–37 with no garment-measurement label. Check its tee pages before using it.
